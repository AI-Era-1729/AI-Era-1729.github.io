Most engineering teams don't fail because they write bad code; they fail because they build exactly what was asked for without testing the core assumption.

When we hand engineers a finalized Figma file, we treat software design like a conveyor belt. We spend three weeks writing clean TypeScript, only to find users get stuck on step two.

Shortening the discovery loop means bringing engineers into research early. Watching a user struggle with a prototype helps developers spot simple UI fixes before writing backend services. (My own early response to feedback was usually just creating another Jira ticket instead of asking why the feature existed.)

The trade-off is focus. If you react to every user interview immediately, your architecture becomes a mess of half-baked feature flags. Test hypotheses aggressively, but protect your build sprint once scope is locked.

This week, invite one engineer to sit in on a live customer call. Don't ask them for a solution; just ask what surprised them about how the product was used.
