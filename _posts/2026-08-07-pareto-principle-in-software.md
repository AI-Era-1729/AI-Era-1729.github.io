Most software teams spend 80% of their sprint cycles building features that less than 5% of their users ever click.

Early Nintendo designers built Super Mario Bros around one core mechanic: the jump. They didn't build twenty complex power-ups first. They spent months tuning momentum, gravity, and landing physics until jumping felt good on its own.

In web development, we do the exact opposite. We over-engineer modular dashboard settings, obscure export formats, and custom filter builders before the primary user flow even works reliably. (My own team once spent three weeks perfecting an advanced search modal that got hit four times a month in production.)

The Pareto principle isn't an excuse to ignore edge cases. The trade-off is that if your core path is clunky, polished fringe features won't save your product retention anyway.

This week, check your analytics for the top two user actions in your web app. Cut one low-traffic backlog item and spend those hours tightening latency on those main flows instead.
