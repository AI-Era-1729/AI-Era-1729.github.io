Researchers at Yale School of Public Health released a preprint study modeling the effects of a Medicare for All–type universal health coverage in the United States. The model predicts the system would cut annual health expenditures by about $1.04 trillion and prevent roughly 114,000 deaths each year.

## Why it matters

Software engineers building health‑tech platforms could see reduced demand for costly administrative and billing integrations as waste is stripped out. Lower drug‑price and fraud‑reduction assumptions imply fewer complex pricing APIs and compliance checks. A shift to universal coverage may drive new data‑sharing standards for dental and preventive services, affecting system design and interoperability requirements.

## The key fact

The study estimates universal coverage would reduce annual health expenditures by $1.04 trillion, nearly 20% of current spending, while averting 114,174 deaths per year.

## Context

The analysis builds on earlier Yale research published in The Lancet (2020) that projected $450 billion in savings and 68,000 lives saved, reflecting rising U.S. health costs and widening gaps between commercial and Medicare payment rates.
