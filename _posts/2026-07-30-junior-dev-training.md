AI didn't kill the need for junior engineers; it erased the easy boilerplate tasks we used to train them on.

Teams are cutting entry-level roles because Copilot scaffolds React components and REST endpoints in seconds. It looks like a win on a spreadsheet. But when you automate simple bugs, you destroy the exact training ground where engineers learn how systems break.

In my team, we noticed seniors drowning in reviews while juniors struggled to parse complex diffs. (I spent my first three months in tech fixing CSS layout bugs, and that tedious work built my mental model.) The risk now is juniors generating code they can't actually debug when the LLM hallucinates.

The solution isn't stopping junior hiring. It's changing what we delegate. Shift junior work from typing boilerplate to reading diffs, writing edge-case tests, and tracing logs.

This week, hand a junior a small ticket with Copilot turned on, but make the acceptance criteria explaining every line of generated code during review.
