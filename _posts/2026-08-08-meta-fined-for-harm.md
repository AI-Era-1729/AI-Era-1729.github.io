Designing for raw retention without safety guardrails is no longer an abstract debate; it is a financial liability.

The $567M verdict against Meta isn't just a legal story. It changes how engineering teams must evaluate product specs. For years, we optimized for infinite scroll and notification loops because DAU was easy to track in Amplitude.

When product asks for sticky UX, missing safety features are rarely treated as blocking bugs. (I've built push schedules myself that hit retention targets while clearly exhausting real users.) Guardrails usually end up buried in a backlogged Jira epic.

The trade-off is real: adding safety friction like quiet hours or usage caps will lower your funnel conversion. But optimizing purely for session length builds systemic risk into your architecture.

This week, look at your top engagement feature. Ask what happens if a user wants to walk away, and pitch one safety boundary to your product manager.
